About

Assistant Professor of Mathematics

I am an Assistant Professor of Mathematics at CHRIST (Deemed to be University), India. I completed my PhD in Mathematics from Banasthali Vidyapith in 2022. My doctoral research focused on the mathematical theory of wavelet transforms, particularly the Mexican Hat Wavelet Transform and its extensions to spaces of generalized functions, distributions and Boehmians.

My research investigates how wavelet transforms can be rigorously defined and analysed beyond classical function spaces. I have worked on representation and inversion theorems, exchange properties, Paley–Wiener–Schwartz-type results, Abelian behaviour, Stieltjes-type transforms and generalized quotient spaces.

Alongside theoretical research, I explore applications of multiscale mathematical methods in biomedical signal analysis, structural damage identification, audio analysis and computational modelling. My objective is to develop methods that remain mathematically interpretable while addressing practical signal-analysis problems.

As an educator, I aim to make mathematics visual, useful and accessible. I use tools such as Desmos, Python, Mentimeter and Google Forms, together with group work, peer learning and project-based activities. I also create instructional videos and digital learning resources to support independent learning.

My academic responsibilities have included teaching, student mentoring, research guidance, curriculum discussions, timetable coordination, conference and workshop organisation, admission duties, outreach activities and doctoral research committee work.

Research

Wavelet analysis & its extensions

My primary research area is wavelet analysis, with particular emphasis on the Mexican Hat Wavelet Transform and its extensions to generalized-function spaces. My work examines the mathematical structure of these transforms, including questions of existence, representation, reconstruction, inversion, asymptotic behaviour and localization.

A second direction of my work concerns Wavelet–Stieltjes transforms, where wavelet-based analysis is combined with Stieltjes integration. This framework is relevant to non-decreasing functions, measures, cumulative processes and generalized signal representations.

I am gradually extending this theoretical foundation toward interdisciplinary problems in which signals contain localized, multiscale or non-stationary features.
